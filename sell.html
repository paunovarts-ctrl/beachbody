What it does

One map, and everything hangs off it.

The beach is built on a drone photo of your actual shoreline, so a bed on the screen is the bed your staff are standing next to.

Live shoreline map

QR check-in

Built for sand

The conditions are the design brief.

Software for a beach fails in ways office software never has to think about. These are not features so much as the reasons this works where a booking website does not.

It works with no signal at all

The whole app is cached on the phone. Coverage on a shoreline is patchy and it cannot be your problem — staff keep scanning and checking in through a dead spot, and it catches up when the bars come back.

It installs like an app, without an app store

Add it to the home screen and it opens full-screen with no browser bar. No store review, no build to ship, no waiting a week to fix something mid-season.

One hand, in direct sunlight

Everything staff reach for all day sits in the bottom third of the screen where a thumb lands, and the default theme is built to be read on a bright beach, not in an office.

In your staff's language

English and Croatian today, switchable mid-shift, and the same work adds another language. Seasonal staff should not have to learn software in a second language as well as the job.

Questions

The ones that get asked first.

Do my guests need to download anything?

No. Guests never install anything and never see the app. This is a tool for the people working the beach — the guest's side of it is a person walking over with a phone, exactly as it is now.

What happens when the signal drops?

Nothing. The app is already on the phone and the day's work is kept there too, so scanning, checking in and the whole map keep working with the network off entirely. When coverage comes back the phones agree with each other again.

Can my staff see the money?

Only if you tick that box for them. Permissions are set per person, and they are enforced by the database rather than by hiding a button — an account without the takings permission cannot read the takings even if someone goes looking.

We already take online bookings. Does this replace that?

No, and it is not trying to. Online booking sells a bed to someone at home; this runs the beach once they arrive. They solve different halves of the day, and most beaches that have the first still run the second on paper.

What if our beach is laid out completely differently?

That is the normal case. The map is your photograph and the spots are placed by hand, so a pier, a terrace, a rocky stretch with eight beds on it and a row of cabanas are all just spots in the right places. Zones are drawn freehand, not chosen from a list.
